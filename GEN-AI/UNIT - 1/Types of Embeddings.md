# Embeddings

- Embeddings are the process of converting data of any type into numerical vectors. These vectors help machine learning and deep learning models understand the meaning, features, and relationships between data.
- Embeddings are not limited to text. They can be used for words, sentences, documents, images, audio, and graph data.
- Different types of embeddings represent different levels of information depending on the input data.

## Types of Embeddings

The major types of embeddings are:

### 1) Word Embeddings

- Word embeddings represent individual words as vectors of numbers in a high-dimensional space.
- They capture the semantic meaning and relationships between words.
- Similar words are placed closer to each other in the vector space.
- They help machines understand similarities between words.

Example: Words like king and queen or cat and kitten have similar semantic relationships.


### 2) Token Embeddings

- Token embeddings convert individual tokens into numerical vectors.
- A token can be a word, subword, or character.
- Tokens are generated by a tokenizer such as BPE or WordPiece.
- Each token is represented by a dense vector containing information about its meaning.

Example: The sentence "Her vocalization was melodic" can be split into tokens such as Her, vocal, ization, was, and melodic. Each token is converted into a vector.


### 3) Sentence Embeddings

- Sentence embeddings represent an entire sentence as a single vector.
- They capture the overall meaning and context of the sentence.
- They help compare sentences based on their meaning rather than just the words used.

Example:
```
"She is happy."

"She is joyful."
```
These sentences have similar meanings, so their embeddings may be close to each other.

### 4) Document Embeddings

- Document embeddings represent an entire document as a single vector.
- They capture the overall meaning and important information of the document.
- They help machines understand and compare large text documents.

**Applications**: Document retrieval, classification, clustering, and information search.

### 5) Graph Embeddings

- Graph embeddings represent nodes in a graph as numerical vectors.
- They capture the relationships, connections, and structural information between nodes.
- They convert complex graph data into a numerical form that machine learning models can process.

Example: In a social network, users are nodes and friendships are edges. Graph embeddings capture the relationships between users.

**Applications**: Network analysis, recommendation systems, and relationship prediction.

### 6) Image Embeddings

- Image embeddings convert images into numerical vectors by extracting important visual features.
- These vectors represent information such as shapes, patterns, objects, and other visual characteristics.
- Similar images can have similar embeddings.

**Applications**: Image classification, object detection, image retrieval, and visual search.

### 7) Audio Embeddings

- Audio embeddings convert audio signals into numerical representations.
- They capture important audio features such as speech patterns, sound characteristics, and musical information.
- They allow machine learning models to process and understand audio data.

**Applications**: Speech recognition, music classification, audio search, and sound detection.

### 8) Positional Embeddings

- Positional embeddings provide information about the position of each token in a sequence.
- They help models understand the order of words in a sentence.
- They are added to token embeddings so that the model can distinguish between words based on their positions.

Example:
```
"The cat sat on the mat."
```
The model needs to understand the order of the words to interpret the sentence correctly.
