# Energy Storage Hybridization in Electric Vehicles

Energy storage hybridization is the process of combining two or more different energy storage systems in an electric vehicle (EV) to improve its overall performance.

A single battery may not be able to provide both high energy for long driving range and high power for sudden acceleration. Therefore, two different energy storage sources are combined to meet these requirements.

For example, a battery and supercapacitor can be used together in an EV.
- Battery provides energy for long-distance driving.
- Supercapacitor provides high power during sudden acceleration.
- Supercapacitor can also store energy recovered during braking.

This combination improves efficiency, battery life and vehicle performance.

## Alternative energy storage sources used in EVs

Different energy sources can be combined based on the vehicle's power and energy requirements. They are:
1. Battery
2. Fuel cell
3. SuperCapacitor
4. Flywheel
5. Hydrogen storage



### 1. Battery

- Stores electrical energy in chemical form.
- Supplies electrical power to the motor to drive the vehicle.
- Provides energy for normal driving and acceleration.
- Can store energy recovered during regenerative braking.
- Example: Lithium-ion battery.

### 2. Fuel cell

- Converts the chemical energy of hydrogen into electrical energy.
- Supplies electricity to the electric motor.
- Produces water and heat during operation in a hydrogen fuel cell.
- Provides continuous power and can increase the driving range.
- Example: Hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles.

### 3. SuperCapacitor

- Stores electrical energy in the form of an electric field.
- Charges and discharges very quickly.
- Supplies high power during sudden acceleration.
- Stores energy during regenerative braking.
- Reduces the load on the battery and increases its life.

### 4. Flywheel

- Stores energy in the form of rotational kinetic energy.
- Stores energy by rotating at high speed.
- Releases energy during acceleration.
- Can store energy during braking.
- Reduces sudden power demand on the battery.

### 5. Hydrogen storage

- Stores hydrogen gas in special high-pressure tanks.
- Hydrogen is supplied to the fuel cell to produce electricity.
- Can be used for long-distance travel.
- Refuelling is faster than charging a large battery.
- Example: Hydrogen fuel-cell cars and buses.
