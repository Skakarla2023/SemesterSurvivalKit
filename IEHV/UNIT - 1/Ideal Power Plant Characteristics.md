- In a vehicle, the power plant is the source that produces the power needed to move the vehicle.
- For a conventional vehicle, the power plant is usually the Internal Combustion Engine (ICE).
- For an electric vehicle, it is the electric motor.

### Ideal power plant

- An ideal power plant is a power source whose torque and power characteristics are perfectly suited to the requirements of the vehicle.
- The ideal characteristic is unchanging power output across the entire operating range.
- The power plant should be able to provide the required power efficiently at both low and high vehicle speeds.

### Why do we need ideal characteristics?

- A vehicle has to perform different tasks.
- For example:
  - Starting from rest
  - Accelerating
  - Climbing a hill
  - Carrying a heavy load
  - Cruising at high speed
- The power requirement is different in each situation.
- So, ideally, the power plant should automatically provide the right amount of torque and speed for each condition.

**Simple example 🚗**

Imagine you are riding a bicycle.

When you start from rest, you need high force (torque) to get the bicycle moving.

Once you are moving fast, you don't need the same high force. Instead, you need the wheels to rotate at a higher speed.

A vehicle power plant has a similar requirement.


### Main Characteristics of an Ideal Power Plant

The ideal traction power plant has two important regions:

**1. Constant Torque at Low Speed**

- At low vehicle speeds, the motor should provide approximately constant/high torque.
- Because high torque is needed to:
  - Start the vehicle from rest
  - Accelerate quickly
  - Climb hills
  - Pull heavy loads
 

**Simple example**

Think about pushing a heavy box.

When the box is stationary, you need a strong push to start moving it.

Similarly, when a car is stationary, it needs high torque to start moving.

**2. Constant Power at Higher Speeds**

- After the vehicle reaches a certain speed, the torque decreases as speed increases, while the power remains approximately constant.

- So, if speed increases, torque can decrease while keeping power approximately constant.

**Simple example**

Imagine riding a bicycle downhill.

At high speed, you don't need the same strong pushing force that you needed to start. Instead, the wheel can rotate faster with less torque.
